Suggestions to retire a symphony that duplicates another or no longer earns its place. Nothing is retired for you: approving records your decision and gives you a checklist to wind the symphony down yourself in Composer.

{# The recommender first: the card to act on is at the top, then the run it came from. #}
Correlated pairs

A symphony is flagged when, over enough shared days, its daily returns track another live symphony's {% if page_copy.screen_threshold is not none %}at a correlation of {{ page_copy.screen_threshold }} or more,{% else %}at or above the correlation screen,{% endif %} even at the low end of the estimate's range and on the worst days, and it has the lower combined return and risk score. Each card names the symphony to retire, then the one to keep after the arrow.

What the terms mean

Deposit-adjusted: the series Composer names deposit-adjusted, which the recommender uses instead of the raw account value series.

Analysed: symphonies with a usable daily series, of all live symphonies.

History: the shortest and longest daily series, in days.

Pairs screened: pairs whose correlation reached the screen, of all pairs compared.

Blocked: screened pairs that did not become a recommendation, counted by the check that stopped them.

Run recommender: runs the nightly analysis now; it takes a minute or two and is refused while the market is open.

kept: the symphony of the pair that stays; the one before the arrow is the one suggested for retirement.

Composite gap: how far the kept symphony's combined return and risk score is ahead of the flagged one's.

Uncertainty gate: passes only if the correlation is still at or above the screen at the low end of its confidence range, so a high reading from a short history does not count.

Structural-redundancy gate: passes only if the two still move together {% if page_copy.stress_fraction_pct is not none %}on the worst {{ page_copy.stress_fraction_pct }} percent of days,{% else %}on their worst days,{% endif %} when diversification matters most.

Approve retirement: records your decision and shows a wind-down checklist; it sells nothing and changes nothing in Composer.

Symphony tenure review

{% if page_copy.tenure_threshold is not none %}Once a symphony has been invested for {{ page_copy.tenure_threshold }} trading days,{% else %}Once a symphony has been invested for the configured minimum number of trading days,{% endif %} this review asks whether it still earns its place: its real results against its own backtest, its effect on the whole portfolio, and whether it duplicates another symphony. Each eligible symphony gets keep, watch, retire-candidate or no verdict; the review only advises, and approving a retirement records your decision without changing anything in Composer.

What the terms mean

Run tenure review: runs the review now instead of waiting for the nightly run; it is refused while the market is open.

trigger: whether the run was the nightly one or one you started. threshold in effect: the trading days a symphony needs before it gets a verdict.

keep: no evidence against it.

watch: it trails its own backtest {% if page_copy.watch_trail_pp is not none %}by more than {{ page_copy.watch_trail_pp }} percentage points of total return,{% else %}by more than the set margin of total return,{% endif %} or the portfolio would have a higher return and no deeper drawdown without it.

retire-candidate: it duplicates another live symphony and scores lower, as under Correlated pairs.

no verdict: there is not yet enough evidence to judge.

basis: which series the figures use, Composer's realized one or the bot's shadow history. missing: evidence items that could not be computed, with the reason.

not applicable: the item does not apply to this symphony, for the reason shown. paired with: the live symphony this one is compared against. normalized across N symphonies: the score is ranked among that many symphonies.

Performance: the symphony's realized return and risk over its tenure.

Composite score: its return and risk score ranked against the other live symphonies.

Redundancy: its return correlation with the other live symphonies.

Backtest: Composer's backtest of its current rules, against what it really earned.

Marginal contribution: the portfolio of eligible symphonies with and without it.

Long VIX hedge: its long-volatility holdings beside the rest of the portfolio, shown for context and not used in the verdict.
